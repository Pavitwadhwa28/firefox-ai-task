# Memo: Does Studying More Actually Help?

**To:** School leadership
**From:** Pavit Wadhwa, 26BCE2551
**Data:** UCI Student Performance, Math file (395 students, grades 0-20)

## What I found
"More study time means better grades" is only weakly true. In the Math data, study time has a small positive link with the final grade (G3): correlation **r = 0.10**, and study time explains only about **1%** of the differences in final grades. Students' earlier grades and past failures matter far more.

## Evidence
- **Study time:** students studying 2-5 hours/week averaged 10.2 out of 20; those studying 5-10 hours averaged 11.4. That is a gain of about 1 mark, with a lot of overlap between groups.
- **Past failures:** r = **-0.36**. Students who failed before tend to score clearly lower, a link about 3-4 times stronger than study time.
- **Previous term grade (G2):** r = **0.90**. G2 alone explains about **82%** of the variation in G3.
- **Portuguese check:** the pattern holds (study time r = 0.25, failures r = -0.39, G2 r = 0.92), but study time matters a bit more there.
- **Robustness:** removing the 38 students with G3 = 0 barely changes the results (study time r = 0.13).

## Advice to students
1. Study does help a little, so don't stop. But hours alone are not a guarantee.
2. Treat your mid-term grades as an early warning. If your period 1 or 2 grade is low, act then, not at the end.
3. If you have failed before, get support early (teachers, tutoring, study groups).
4. Focus on how you study (regular, active practice) and not only how long.

## One claim I chose NOT to make
I did **not** claim that "studying more *causes* higher grades" (nor that studying doesn't matter). This data is observational, and study time is self-reported in just four broad bands, not measured hours. Stronger or more motivated students may simply report studying differently, so the link could run in either direction or come from a third factor. Also, G3 = 0 may mean the student skipped the exam, not that they knew nothing.

## Limitations
One school year, two schools in Portugal, self-reported habits. Results may not apply to other schools or countries.
